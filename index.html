The Five Faces of Permanent Establishment

Permanent Establishment is the threshold concept in international taxation. Without a PE in the source country, business profits of a foreign enterprise can only be taxed in its country of residence (Article 7). With a PE, the source country gets first taxing rights on profits attributable to it. The OECD Model recognises five distinct types — each with its own threshold and case-law jurisprudence.

Type 1 · The Default

Fixed Place PE

Article 5(1)–(4)

A fixed place of business through which the enterprise's business is wholly or partly carried on. Six conditions must concur: existence, location, fixity, disposal, business activity, and non-preparatory nature.

Six concurrent conditions
Type 2 · Time-Based

Construction PE

Article 5(3) · BEPS Action 7

A building site, construction, installation, or assembly project becomes a PE if it lasts beyond a treaty-specific time threshold. Anti-contract-splitting rules apply (BEPS Action 7).

6–12 months (varies by treaty)
Type 3 · India-Specific

Service PE

Article 5(2)(l) (India-style treaties)

Furnishing of services through employees or personnel in source country for > threshold days in 12 months. Physical presence required (Clifford Chance, Delhi HC Dec 2025). Not in OECD Model — UN Model only.

90–183 days · Related party shorter
Type 4 · Personal

Agency PE

Article 5(5)–(6)

A dependent agent who habitually concludes contracts (or plays the principal role leading to conclusion — post-BEPS) creates a PE. Independent agents acting in ordinary course do not.

Conduct-based · No day count
Type 5 · Digital (India)

Significant Economic Presence

Sec 9(1)(i) Expl 2A · 2018

India's domestic-law concept. Not strictly a PE under treaty but creates a deemed business connection. Triggered by transactions ≥ ₹2 crore OR systematic soliciting of ≥ 3 lakh Indian users.

₹2 cr / 3 lakh users
Pillar I · Threshold

Source-State Taxing Right

PE is the trigger that gives the source country the right to tax business profits. Below the threshold, residence country has exclusive rights (Article 7).

Pillar II · Attribution

Only PE Profits Taxed

Even when PE exists, the source country only taxes profits attributable to that PE — calculated as if PE were a separate, independent enterprise dealing at arm's length.

Pillar III · Anti-Abuse

BEPS Tightenings

BEPS Action 7 (2015) and the MLI (Articles 12–15) closed common avoidance routes — commissionnaire arrangements, anti-fragmentation of preparatory activities, contract-splitting on construction sites.

Article 7 Profit Attribution

Once a PE exists, only profits attributable to that PE can be taxed in the source country. The OECD Authorised Approach treats the PE as a separate, independent enterprise dealing at arm's length with the head office. Below is a working worksheet for the basic AOA computation.
